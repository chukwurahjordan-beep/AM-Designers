Retail units bring their own problems: high ambient noise, shopfits that change more often than the building does, and shutters or doors that need to behave correctly in an alarm condition. The system has to keep working through refits, not just at handover.

Why

What a retail system actually has to do

Sounders need to be audible over shop floor noise and stock, escape routes need to stay clear through seasonal layout changes, and any fire shutters, roller doors or access control on the unit need to fail safely in an alarm condition — not just look compliant on the drawing.

How

What we produce

  • →Zone diagrams matched to the unit's actual layout, updated as shopfits change.
  • →Cause & effect schedules covering shutters, fire doors and any interlinked plant.
  • →Sound pressure notes flagged where ambient retail noise affects sounder placement.
  • →As-built updates issued after refits so the wall-mounted diagram never falls out of date.

Commercial buildings — offices, mixed-use blocks, multi-storey premises — carry the highest documentation burden, because life safety, business continuity and insurance all depend on the same drawing set being right.

Why

What's required to achieve a safe system

Commercial premises need the correct category selected against BS 5839-1 (typically L1–L3 depending on escape route complexity and occupancy), phased or simultaneous evacuation strategy defined for multi-storey buildings, and every interface — access control, AOVs, dampers, lift recall — mapped so the cause & effect matches what the fire strategy actually requires, not a generic template.

How

What we produce

  • →Category justification — the reasoning behind L1/L2/L3 selection, referenced against the fire strategy.
  • →Full cause & effect matrices covering detection, sounders, dampers, AOVs, lift recall and access control interfaces.
  • →Phased evacuation zone diagrams for multi-storey buildings, cross-checked against escape routes.
  • →Commissioning-ready documentation handed over in the format building control and the fire risk assessor expect.

Domestic and residential work runs on BS 5839-6 rather than -1, and the biggest risk isn't complexity — it's a system that's over- or under-specified for the type of dwelling, or documentation a landlord can't produce when asked for it.

Why

What a residential system needs to achieve

The right grade and category (LD1, LD2 or LD3) depends on the size and layout of the dwelling and whether it's a single household, an HMO, or converted flats — get it wrong and either occupants are under-protected or the landlord is paying for detection they don't need.

How

What we produce

  • →Grade & category selection against BS 5839-6, matched to dwelling type and occupancy.
  • →Zone diagrams for HMOs and converted properties where multiple households share a system.
  • →As-built records landlords and letting agents can produce on request at inspection.
  • →Interlink schematics for mains-wired detector networks across multiple floors.
